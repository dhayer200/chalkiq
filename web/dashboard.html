Elo Rating System

Every team starts at 1500. After each game, ratings update:

R' = R + K × MOV_factor × (outcome - expected)

Where outcome is 1 for a win, 0 for a loss, and expected is the pre-game win probability.

Win Probability

The probability of Team A beating Team B:

P(A wins) = 1 / (1 + 10^((R_B - R_A - HCA) / scale))

HCA is the home-court/field advantage in Elo points. scale controls how quickly probability changes with rating gaps (300 for NCAAB, 350 for NBA, 400 for MLB).

Margin of Victory

Blowouts carry more weight, but with autocorrelation correction (FiveThirtyEight method):

MOV_factor = ln(|margin| + 1) × 2.2 / (gap × 0.001 + 2.2)

The denominator prevents a team from getting too much credit for blowing out a weak opponent they were already expected to crush.

Tempo Normalization

For basketball, margin of victory is normalized by game pace. A 10-point win in a 60-possession game is worth more than in an 80-possession game. Estimated possessions:

poss = 0.5 × (FGA - ORB + TO + 0.475 × FTA)

If possessions are below average (~70), the margin is scaled up; if above, scaled down. MLB does not use tempo normalization.

Season Regression

Between seasons, all ratings regress toward 1500 to account for roster turnover:

R_new = R_old - regress × (R_old - 1500)

NCAAB uses 33% regression (heavy turnover). NBA uses 25%. MLB uses 20% (most roster continuity).

Closing Line Value (CLV)

The ultimate measure of a betting model. CLV compares the model's pre-game probability to the closing line set by the market (DraftKings, FanDuel, etc.).

CLV = model_prob - closing_implied_prob

Positive CLV means the model identified value that the market hadn't priced in. Consistently beating closing lines is the hallmark of a sharp model — closing lines incorporate all public and sharp money, making them the most efficient prices available.

Log Loss & Brier Score

Log Loss (cross-entropy) heavily penalizes confident wrong predictions. A coin-flip baseline scores 0.6931. Lower is better.

LL = -[y × ln(p) + (1-y) × ln(1-p)]

Brier Score is mean squared error between prediction and outcome. Baseline is 0.2500. Lower is better.
